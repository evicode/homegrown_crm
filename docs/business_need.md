Tim, I looked through Dreamsmith Labs, and I think there’s a fairly clear answer.

Your problem isn't that the site looks like a mediocre dev shop. Quite the opposite. The positioning is unusually strong: “Custom technology for original thinkers,” “bespoke product experience,” “built end-to-end,” “from first principles.”

The problem is distribution and commercial specificity.

Right now Dreamsmith Labs tells me who you are beautifully. It doesn't yet give a buyer enough reasons to think, “These are the people I need for the problem I have right now.”

And I would not respond by turning Dreamsmith into another fucking:

We leverage cutting-edge cloud-native technologies to deliver scalable digital transformation solutions.

There are already 900 billion companies saying that.

I think your best route is three parallel pipelines

1. Agency/consultancy partnerships — fastest route to revenue

This would be my #1 priority.

Don't primarily hunt companies that need an app. Hunt companies that already have clients who occasionally need an app.

Think:

branding/design agencies
UX studios
marketing agencies
creative agencies
fractional CTOs/CIOs
IT/MSP firms
business consultancies
AI consultancies without serious application-development capability
web agencies that occasionally get projects beyond their technical depth

They already have customer acquisition. They already have trust. Then a client says:

“Could we turn this into an app?”

or

“We need this to integrate with our ERP.”

or

“Can we build an AI system around this?”

And the agency thinks: Fuck. We don't do that.

You want Dreamsmith Labs to be the phone call they make.

Offer white-label, co-branded, and referral arrangements. You don't even necessarily need them to give up the client relationship.

This is especially attractive because your portfolio isn't just theoretical development. You've dealt with mobile, cloud infrastructure, ecommerce, payment systems, third-party APIs, AI integration, data pipelines, weird bespoke business systems, etc. That's exactly the kind of “we need somebody who can figure this shit out” capability that partner agencies can sell.

Partnerships and referrals also consistently show up as some of the strongest acquisition channels for small software agencies, while indiscriminate cold outreach performs substantially worse.

2. Founder-led targeted outbound — but NOT “Need software?” spam

I'd build a list of perhaps 100–200 companies, not 20,000 email addresses.

And don't target “companies that might need software.”

Target observable problems.

For example:

Company has an ancient customer portal.

You contact the COO/CTO:

I was looking at your customer workflow and noticed X. We recently solved a similar problem involving Y. I have an idea for simplifying this without replacing your existing system. Happy to sketch it out if it's useful.

That's a radically different proposition from:

Hello {{first_name}}, Dreamsmith Labs is an industry-leading software development company...

Straight into the fucking sun.

Modern agency advice is converging on this distinction: narrow targeting based on actual relevance/intent rather than mass personalized spam.

Your engineering background is an advantage here because you can identify technical pain a salesperson wouldn't recognize.

Look for triggers like a company launching a product, receiving funding, hiring several engineers, posting a CTO vacancy, complaining about legacy systems, expanding ecommerce, launching mobile, acquiring another company, implementing an ERP/CRM, adding AI functionality, or replacing an existing vendor.

Those are buying signals.

The sales call

I wouldn't run traditional agency sales calls.

Spend maybe 80% of the conversation asking questions.

What are you trying to accomplish?

Why now?

How does it work currently?

What's painful?

What have you already tried?

Who uses it?

What systems does it touch?

What happens if nothing changes?

Then eventually:

I think I understand the problem.

And explain it back to them.

If you can describe their problem more clearly than they described it to you, you've just demonstrated something extremely valuable.

The close

That makes Discovery itself valuable rather than a hostage situation designed to force the larger contract.

And paradoxically, I suspect that makes them much more likely to have Dreamsmith build it.
